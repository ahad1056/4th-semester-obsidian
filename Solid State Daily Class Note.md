## Classification of Solid Materials
- Solid materials are categorized into main types.
- Crystalline (which includes Single crystal).
- Polycrystalline.
- Amorphous.


## Crystal Dimensions and Single Crystals
- **1D Structure:** Represented as a single linear arrangement of atoms.
  - It exhibits periodicity.
  - It extends to infinity.
  - This continuous periodic structure is also called a Single crystal.

## Polycrystalline Materials
- Polycrystalline materials consist of multiple localized crystals.
- These individual localized crystals are called "grains".
- The interface or border where these different grains meet is called the "grain boundary" or "junction".

## 🧭 Crystal Directions, Planes & Key Topics
The class introduced core solid-state crystallography concepts, including crystal directions and planes[^1], and listed foundational topics to study:
- **Cubic Crystal Structures:** Simple Cubic (SC, written as `SC`), Body-Centered Cubic (BCC), and Face-Centered Cubic (FCC)
- **Other Crystal Systems:** Monoclinic (`Mono`) and the 7 crystal systems overall
- **Structural Terms:** Crystal[^2], crystal structure[^3], lattice[^4], lattice point[^5], basis[^6], unit cell[^7], primitive and non-primitive cells[^8], grain[^9], grain boundary[^10], amorphous[^11], and Bravais lattice[^12]

---

## 🧱 Basis Vector & Unit Cells
- **Basis Vector:** Defines the position of the basis (atoms/motifs) relative to one another within a crystal structure.
- **Types of Unit Cells:**
  1. **Primitive Cell:** The smallest-volume unit cell of a crystal that contains a single effective lattice point (only corner lattice points contribute, each shared among adjacent cells so total lattice points per cell = $1$).
     - *Correction note:* A primitive cell always has lattice points at its corners (and contains a net total of **1** lattice point per unit cell).
  2. **Non-Primitive Cell:** A conventional unit cell that contains **more than one** lattice point per unit cell (for example, BCC has 2 effective lattice points and FCC has 4 effective lattice points).

---

## 🌐 Bravais Lattices & 3D Crystal Systems
- **Three-Dimensional Classification:** There are **7 crystal systems** (cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, rhombohedral/trigonal, hexagonal, monoclinic, and triclinic) and **14 distinct 3D Bravais lattices**.

## 📖 Key Definitions (Footnotes)
[^1]: **Crystal Direction & Plane:** Specific crystallographic orientations and atomic planes within a crystal lattice, commonly denoted using indices such as $[uvw]$ for directions and $(hkl)$ for planes.
[^2]: **Crystal:** A solid material whose constituent atoms, ions, or molecules are arranged in an ordered, periodic, three-dimensional pattern.
[^3]: **Crystal Structure:** The combination of a crystal lattice (periodic point array) and a basis (the group of atoms assigned to each lattice point).
[^4]: **Lattice (Space Lattice):** A periodic, infinite three-dimensional array of points in space where every point has an identical surrounding environment.
[^5]: **Lattice Point:** An individual point in a space lattice that represents a position with identical local surroundings throughout the crystal.
[^6]: **Basis:** A single atom or group of atoms placed at each lattice point to form the physical crystal structure.
[^7]: **Unit Cell:** The basic structural building block of a crystal lattice whose repetition in three dimensions by translation reproduces the entire crystal.
[^8]: **Primitive vs. Non-Primitive Cell:** A primitive cell contains only one lattice point per unit cell (the minimum volume cell for a given lattice), whereas a non-primitive cell contains two or more lattice points per unit cell for geometric convenience or higher symmetry.
[^9]: **Grain:** A single, small crystal (crystallite) with a uniform atomic orientation inside a polycrystalline material.
[^10]: **Grain Boundary:** The narrow interfacial region separating two adjacent grains of different crystallographic orientations in a polycrystalline solid.
[^11]: **Amorphous Solid:** A non-crystalline solid that lacks long-range atomic periodicity and order (similar to a liquid's frozen arrangement).
[^12]: **Bravais Lattice:** One of the 14 unique three-dimensional periodic point arrangements generated by combining the 7 crystal systems with centering types (primitive, body-centered, face-centered, and base-centered).

## Class Notes: Solid State Electronics - Miller Indices & Crystallographic Concepts (Nov 25, 2026)

## 📐 Miller Indices Overview
Miller indices[^13] are used to specify crystal directions (lattice point vectors from an origin) and crystal planes in a crystal system.

- **Bracket Conventions:**
  - $[hkl]$ or $[uvw]$: A specific **crystal direction** (written in square brackets; for lattice points/directions from an origin).
  - $\langle hkl \rangle$ or $\langle uvw \rangle$: A **family of equivalent directions** (written in angle brackets).
  - $(hkl)$: A specific **crystal plane** (written in parentheses / `plane এর জন্য ( )`).
  - $\{hkl\}$: A **family of equivalent planes** (written in curly braces).
- **Cubic Geometry Reference:** A cube has **6 faces**, **8 corners**, and **12 edges** (and in a cubic system, lattice parameters satisfy $a = b = c$ and interfacial angles satisfy $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 90^\circ$).
- **Reduction & Sign Rules:**
  - Miller indices are always reduced to the **smallest integer values** (`Miller indices always minimum ride আসবে` / `এই point কে যাতে ছোট করা যায় কাটাকাটি করে, তা করব` — e.g., scaling down `[4 2]` by a common factor of $2$ gives `[2 1]`).
  - For a **negative direction** along an axis, place a **bar** over the index rather than a minus sign (e.g., $\bar{2}$ instead of $-2$: `-ve দিকে move করতে দেখলে ¯ দিয়ে দেখানো হয়`).
  - **Correction Note:** In one of the handwritten 2D examples, a vector written as $\vec{r} = n_1 \vec{a} - n_2 \vec{b} = 3$ is simplified to show the index pair $[3\;\bar{2}]$. Note that a position vector is a linear combination of basis vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ (not a single scalar equal to $3$); the components along $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ (reduced to smallest integers) form the direction indices $[h\;\bar{k}]$.

---

## 📏 1D Lattice Direction Example
Consider a 1D array of lattice points separated by the lattice parameter (unit distance) $\vec{a}$:
- If point $P$ is taken as the **origin** and the displacement vector to point $Q$ is $\vec{r} = n\vec{a} = 2\vec{a}$, then the Miller direction index is:
  $$[h] = [2]$$
- If the origin is reversed to $Q$ and the vector points back to $P$, then $\vec{r} = -2\vec{a}$, giving:
  $$[h] = [\bar{2}]$$

---

## 🧊 3D Crystal Planes & Hexagonal Indices
To determine the Miller indices $(hkl)$ of a **crystal plane** in 3D:
1. **Find Intercepts:** Determine where the plane intersects the $x$, $y$, and $z$ axes in units of the lattice parameters ($a, b, c$). If a plane is parallel to an axis, its intercept is $\infty$ (`for parallel, intersect ∞`).
2. **Take Reciprocals:** Invert each intercept value (`তারপর reciprocal করব` / `find reciprocal`).
3. **Clear Fractions to Smallest Integers:** Multiply through to clear any fractions so that $h, k, l$ are the smallest integers (`ভগ্নাংশ আসলে গুন করে integer করব`).
   - *Example from notes:* For intercepts $(1, \infty, \infty)$, taking reciprocals gives $\left(\frac{1}{1}, \frac{1}{\infty}, \frac{1}{\infty}\right) = (1, 0, 0)$ (since $\frac{1}{\infty} = 0$).

- **Hexagonal Crystal System (4-Index Notation):**
  For hexagonal crystals, four indices are used: $(hkil)$ for planes and $[uvtw]$ (written as `[h k i l]` in the notes) for directions, where $i = -(h + k)$ or $t = -(u + v)$.

## 📖 Key Definitions (Footnotes)
[^13]: **Miller Indices:** A standard notation system in crystallography consisting of integers used to uniquely identify specific crystal planes $(hkl)$ and crystal directions $[uvw]$ within a crystal lattice.
[^14]: **Packing Fraction (Atomic Packing Factor, APF):** The fraction of total unit cell volume occupied by constituent atoms (modeled as hard spheres), assuming adjacent atoms touch along a close-packed direction.
[^15]: **Bragg's Law:** The diffraction condition given by $2d\sin\theta = n\lambda$, relating interplanar spacing ($d$), glancing/Bragg angle ($\theta$), diffraction order ($n$), and X-ray wavelength ($\lambda$) for constructive interference.
[^16]: **Binding Energy (Cohesive Energy):** The net energy released when isolated constituent atoms/ions assemble into a stable crystal lattice from infinite separation (or equivalently, the energy required to disassemble the crystal into isolated constituents).
[^17]: **XRD (X-Ray Diffraction):** A non-destructive characterization technique that uses scattered X-rays to determine crystal structure, lattice parameters, phase composition, and interplanar spacing.
