Class Notes: Communication

📌 Core Definitions

- Message: Raw data which is sent by the sender for the receiver.
- Information: Organized data, or meaningful data.
- Sender: Initiator, encoder, transmitter.
- Source: Originator.

📡 Bandwidth Requirements

Standard bandwidth ranges for different types of signals:

- Speech: 300 Hz to 3400 Hz
- Music: Up to 20 kHz
- Video: 4.2 MHz
- TV: 6 MHz

🔊 Noise[^1] Classification

Noise is categorized into two main types:

#### 1. Internal Noise:[^2]

- Thermal noise[^3]
- Shot noise[^4]
- Flicker noise[^5]

#### 2. External Noise:

- Atmospheric noise
- Cosmic noise
- Manmade noise

🧮 Signal to Noise Ratio & Noise Figure

- Signal to noise ratio: Measured at a certain point.
- Noise Figure Calculation:
  - F = (Input S/N ratio) / (Output S/N ratio)

[^1]: **Definition of Noise:** In communication systems, **noise** is defined as an unwanted electrical signal that interferes with the transmission, reception, and processing of the desired information signal.
[^2]: Internal noise is generated by the active and passive electrical components (such as resistors, diodes, and transistors) within the communication circuit itself.
[^3]: **Thermal Noise (Johnson-Nyquist Noise)**
	
	- **Cause:** It is caused by the random, thermally induced motion of free electrons inside a conductor.
	    
	- **Characteristics:** The intensity of this noise is directly proportional to the absolute temperature. It is present in all passive resistive components and is uniformly distributed across the frequency spectrum (White Noise).
[^4]: **B. Shot Noise**
	
	- **Cause:** It arises due to the discrete (particle-like) nature of charge carriers (electrons or holes) moving across a junction or potential barrier (e.g., in a P-N junction diode or transistor).
	    
	- **Characteristics:** Instead of flowing continuously, electrons arrive at a destination randomly, creating fluctuations in the current. It is compared to the sound of lead shots falling on a metal sheet.
[^5]: **C. Flicker Noise (1/f Noise)**
	
	- **Cause:** It is primarily caused by structural defects, impurities, and surface imperfections in semiconductor materials.
	    
	- **Characteristics:** It is highly significant at lower frequencies and its power decreases inversely as the frequency increases (hence the name 1/f noise).
