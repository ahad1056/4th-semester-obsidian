### Definition of Contract

According to Section 2(h) of the Contract Act, 1872, "**An agreement enforceable by law is a**
**contract**." Thus, every contract is an agreement, but every agreement is not a contract. An agreement becomes a contract only when it is recognized and enforceable by law. 

According to Section 2(e), an agreement is formed by a promise or a set of promises that form
consideration for each other

#### Essential Elements of a Valid Contract
1. Offer and Acceptance[^1]
2. Intention to Create Legal Relationship
3. Lawful Consideration[^2]
4. Capacity of Parties[^3]
5. Free Consent
6. Lawful Object
7. Certainty of Terms
8. Possibility of Performance
9. Not Expressly Declared Void








































[^1]: There must be a lawful offer by one party and a lawful acceptance by the other party. The
	
	acceptance must be absolute, unconditional, and communicated properly. Offer and acceptance
	
	together create an agreement.
[^2]: No consideration, no contract.
[^3]: The parties must be competent to contract. According to Section 11, a person is competent if he:
	
	● Has attained the age of majority
	
	● Is of sound mind
	
	● Is not disqualified by any law
	
	A contract made by an incompetent person is not valid.
