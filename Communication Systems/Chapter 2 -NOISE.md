# 2.2.1 Thermal Agitation Noise

> [!info] Core Concept
> **Thermal Noise** (also called *agitation noise*, *white noise*, or *Johnson noise*) is random electrical noise generated inside a resistor due to the rapid, random thermal motion of atoms and electrons.

### Most Important Points
* **Temperature Dependence:** Particle kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature ($T$). At absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$ or $-273^\circ\text{C}$), thermal motion ceases and noise power drops to zero.

* **Frequency Independence:** Noise power is evenly distributed across the entire frequency spectrum (*white noise*). It depends on the **bandwidth ($\Delta f$)**, not on the operating center frequency.

* **Signal Masking:** An amplifier or receiver cannot detect input signals weaker than or comparable to its input thermal noise voltage ($V_n$), as the signal gets masked and lost.

---

### Maximum Noise Power ($P_n$)
The maximum thermal noise power generated by a resistor is proportional to absolute temperature and bandwidth:

$$P_n = k T \Delta f$$

Where:
* $P_n$ = Maximum noise power output ($\text{Watts}$)
* $k$ = Boltzmann's constant ($1.38 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J/K}$)
* $T$ = Absolute temperature in Kelvin ($\text{K} = 273 + ^\circ\text{C}$)
* $\Delta f$ = Bandwidth of interest ($\text{Hz}$)

---

### Derivation of RMS Noise Voltage ($V_n$)

![[Chapter 1 - Introduction to Communication Systems & Signals-1794657382978.webp|239]]
              *(Fig. 2.1: Resistance noise generator circuit diagram here)*

1. **Equivalent Circuit:** A noisy resistor is modeled as an AC noise voltage source ($V_n$) in series with a noiseless internal resistor ($R$), connected to a noiseless load resistor ($R_L$).
2. **Maximum Power Transfer:** For maximum noise power transfer from $R$ to $R_L$, set $R_L = R$. Under this condition, the voltage across the load ($V$) is half the noise voltage:
   $$V = \frac{V_n}{2}$$
3. **Power Across Load:**
   $$P_n = \frac{V^2}{R_L} = \frac{V^2}{R} = \frac{(V_n / 2)^2}{R} = \frac{V_n^2}{4R}$$
4. **Solving for $V_n^2$:**
   $$V_n^2 = 4 R P_n$$
5. **Substituting $P_n = k T \Delta f$:**
   $$V_n^2 = 4 k T \Delta f R$$
6. **Final RMS Noise Voltage Equation:**
   $$V_n = \sqrt{4 k T \Delta f R}$$

> [!tip] Key Takeaway
> From $V_n = \sqrt{4 k T \Delta f R}$, the square of the RMS noise voltage ($V_n^2$) is directly proportional to **temperature ($T$)**, **bandwidth ($\Delta f$)**, and **resistance ($R$)**.
