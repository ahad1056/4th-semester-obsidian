## **Section: A**

**1.** `[(1.5+1.5+3+1+2 = 9)]`
a. What limits our ability to communicate? How this limitation also adds extra benefit to the communication system?
b. What is the need for modulation in a communication system?
c. Discuss the types, cause and effects of the various forms of noise which may be created within a receiver or an amplifier.
d. Sketch AM wave for modulation indices of $m = 0.5$ and $m = 1$, when $m(t) = B\cos\omega_m t$.
e. Calculate the percentage of power saving when the carrier and one of the sideband is suppressed in an AM wave modulated to a depth of (i) $100\%$ and (ii) $50\%$.

**2.** `[(1.5+2.5+3+2 = 9)]`
a. Define noise. Where is it most likely to affect the signal?
b. Explain the principle of nonlinear modulator. Why it is called a single balance modulator?
c. Prove that the balanced modulator produces an output consisting of sidebands only with the carrier removed.
d. Why the efficiency of SSB+C is pathetically lower than the AM in case of envelope detection.

**3.** `[(2+2+3+2 = 9)]`
a. Of the various advantages of FM over AM, identify and discuss those due to the intrinsic qualities of frequency modulation.
b. With the aid of vector diagram, explain what happens when a carrier is modulated by a single noise frequency.
c. Draw the complete block diagram of the Armstrong frequency modulation system and explain the functions of the mixer and multipliers. In what circumstances can we dispense with the mixer?
d. Find the carrier and modulating frequencies, the modulation index and the maximum deviation of the FM wave represented by the voltage equation $v = 12\sin(6 \times 10^8 t + 5\sin 1250t)$. What power will this FM wave dissipate in a $10\,\Omega$ resistor?

**4.** `[(2+3+2+2 = 9)]`
a. Draw the block diagram of AM transmitter and explain the function of each block.
b. Illustrate the functional diagram of a Superheterodyne AM receiver.
c. What is pre-emphasis? Why it is used? Explain why de-emphasis must be used also.
d. Calculate the image-frequency of a receiver having RF amplifier and an IF of $450\text{ kHz}$, if the $Q$s of the relevant coils are $65$, at an incoming frequency of
   i) $1200\text{ kHz}$,
   ii) $20\text{ MHz}$.

## **Section: B**

**5.** `[(2+3+1+3 = 9)]`
a. Define luminance and chrominance signal.
b. Draw a scanning pattern interlaced in two fields for a total of 25 lines per frame. Also show the corresponding sawtooth waveforms for horizontal and vertical scanning.
c. What is persistence of vision? What is the impact of persistence of vision of TV broadcasting.
d. Sketch and explain the electron gun arrangement for three different types of colour picture tube.

**6.** `[(2.5+2+2+2.5 = 9)]`
a. Describe ground-wave propagation. What is angle of tilt? How does it affect field strength at a distance from the transmitter?
b. Describe in some detail the main abnormal ionospheric variations.
c. Define skip distance, and show how it is related to the maximum usable frequency.
d. Describe the earth curvature on space wave propagation.

**7.** `[(3+1.5+3+1.5 = 9)]`
a. Drive the basic radar range equation, as governed by the minimum receivable echo power $P_{\min}$.
b. Write the advantages, applications and limitations of continuous wave Doppler radar?
c. Draw a functional block diagram of pulsed radar set, and describe the function of each block.
d. Calculate the minimum receivable signal in a radar receiver which has an IF bandwidth of $1.5\text{ MHz}$ and a $9\text{-dB}$ noise figure.

**8.** `[(2+2+2+3 = 9)]`
a. Define elevation angle, inclination angle and visible angle.
b. State and explain Kepler's laws of planetary motion.
c. What do you mean by transponder? Describe the basics about the transponder of satellite communication system.
d. Find out the radius of the geostationary satellite.

## **Section-A**

**1.** `[2+1+4+2 = 9]`
a. What are the basic constituents of a communication system?
b. The carrier performs certain functions in radio communications. What are they?
c. Discuss the types, causes and effects of the various forms of noise which may be created within a receiver or an amplifier.
d. Unmodulated RF carrier power of $2\text{ kW}$ sends a current of $5\text{ amp}$ rms through an antenna. On amplitude modulation by another sinusoidal voltage, the antenna current increases to $5.9\text{ amp}$. Calculate the modulation index, and carrier power after modulation.

**2.** `[(3+3+1+2) = 9]`
a. What is amplitude modulation (AM)? With necessary expressions find the bandwidth of an AM signal?
b. Describe double sideband – suppressed carrier (DSB-SC) and its advantages over AM modulation.
c. What are the applications of amplitude modulation and frequency modulation?
d. Calculate the percentage of power saving in AM wave when
   i) The carrier is suppressed
   ii) One of the sidebands suppressed.

**3.** `[(2+3+3+1) = 9]`
a. Define blanking and synchronizing pulses. What are the functions of these pulses in television?
b. Why is scanning necessary in television transmission? Write short notes on horizontal and vertical scanning.
c. Draw a scanning pattern interlaced in two fields for a total of 21 lines per frame. Also show the corresponding sawtooth waveforms for horizontal and vertical scanning.
d. What are the effects of incorrect aspect ratio?

**4.** `[(3+2+3+1) = 9]`
a. Describe briefly the strata of ionosphere and their effects on sky-wave propagation.
b. What is the secant law of wave propagation?
c. Describe in some detail the main abnormal ionospheric variations.
d. Two points on earth are $1500\text{ km}$ apart and are to communicate by means of HF. Given that this is to be a single-hop transmission, the critical frequency at that time is $7\text{ MHz}$ and conditions are idealized, calculate the MUF for those two points if the height of the ionospheric layer is $300\text{ km}$.

## **SECTION – B**

**5.** `[(3+2+2+2) = 9]`
a. Derive an expression for the instantaneous value of an FM signal and define its modulation index.
b. Write down the salient features of wideband and narrowband FM signal.
c. With the aid of vector diagrams explain what happens when a carrier is modulated by signal noise frequency.
d. A $25\text{ MHz}$ carrier is modulated by a $400\text{ Hz}$ sine wave. If the carrier voltage is $4\text{ V}$ and the maximum deviation is $10\text{ kHz}$, write the equation of this modulated wave for (i) FM and (ii) PM. If the modulating frequency is now changed to $2\text{ kHz}$, all else remaining constant, write a new equation for (i) FM and (ii) PM.

**6.** `[(3+2+2+2) = 9]`
a. Draw the block diagram of FM transmitter and explain the function of each block.
b. Explain how the constant intermediate frequency is achieved in the SHR.
c. What is pre-emphasis? Why it is used? Explain why de-emphasis must be used also.
d. In a broadcast superheterodyne receiver having no RF amplifier, the loaded $Q$ of the antenna coupling circuit (at the input to the mixer) is $100$. If the intermediate frequency is $455\text{ kHz}$, calculate the image frequency and its rejection ratio (a) at $1000\text{ kHz}$ and (b) at $25\text{ MHz}$.

**7.** `[(3+1.5+1.5+3) = 9]`
a. Discuss the reflection mechanism whereby electromagnetic waves are bent back by a layer of the ionosphere. Include in your discussion a description of the virtual height of a layer.
b. How do radio waves propagate through space?
c. What is the formula for radio horizon range?
d. Derive the basic radar range equation, as governed by the minimum receivable echo power $P_{\min}$.

**8.** `[(2+2+3+2) = 9]`
a. What is the difference between a geosynchronous and a geostationary satellite orbit? What are the conditions required for orbit to be geostationary?
b. Explain equatorial, polar and inclined orbits.
c. Briefly describe the function of each component of a communication satellite transponder.
d. Determine the total noise power for a receiver with an input bandwidth of $20\text{ MHz}$ and an equivalent noise temperature of $600\text{ K}$.
